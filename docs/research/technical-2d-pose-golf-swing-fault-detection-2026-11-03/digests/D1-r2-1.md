# D1-r2-1: Local 2D pose on DTL golf video, round 2 (verification + new leads)

- Run: technical-2d-pose-golf-swing-fault-detection-2026-11-03, round 2, digest 1
- Accessed: 2026-11-03
- Decision served: what the PRD can promise on processing speed (a 10 s clip at 120–240 fps, so 1,200–2,400 frames, analysed in under 2 min on a laptop CPU) and on measurement confidence
- Tool budget: 18 calls used. 8 distinct sources were read directly, plus search-result summaries.

## Verification

- **V1. RTMPose-m with detector at about 26.6 ms/frame on an i7-11700, single thread: UNVERIFIED. Nothing independent was found.**
  - The rtmlib README (Tau-J, https://github.com/Tau-J/rtmlib) has no speed benchmarks at all. It lists only accuracy and backends.
  - A search snippet pointed to the MMPose `projects/rtmpose/benchmark` folder (a gitlink.org.cn mirror of OpenMMLab). It reportedly shows a pose-only ORT run of RTMPose-m 256x192 at about 8.7–11.2 ms per call on the same i7-11700, and the claim "90+ FPS on an Intel i7-11700 CPU".
  - That source has the same publisher (OpenMMLab) and the same CPU as the paper, so it is not independent. I did not read it directly; it was a search snippet only.
  - The numbers are consistent with the paper if the detector accounts for most of the 26.6 ms. No laptop-CPU figure was found.
- **V2. YOLO pose nano at about 40 ms/frame on CPU with ONNX: UNVERIFIED.**
  - The only numbers found were for YOLOv8n *detection*, not pose: about 39.9 ms/img with ONNX and about 11.3 ms/img with OpenVINO on a 4th-gen Xeon at FP32.
  - These came from mirrors of the Ultralytics OpenVINO docs (dagshub / Hugging Face copies, https://dagshub.com/Ultralytics/ultralytics/src/v8.2.97/docs/en/integrations/openvino.md). Same publisher, not independent, server CPU, search snippet only.
  - No independent laptop i5/i7 pose benchmark was found.

## Claims

1. The BlazePose GHUM model card gives CPU speeds on a Pixel 3 (XNNPack TFLite): Lite about 44 FPS, Full about 18 FPS, Heavy about 4 FPS. On TFLite GPU the speeds are about 49, 40 and 19 FPS. Model sizes are 3 MB, 6 MB and 26 MB.
   - Source: https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf | Google | 2021-04 | accessed 2026-11-03
   - Confidence: high that the card says this. These are phone figures, not laptop figures.
   - Class: performance
   - Derived arithmetic (mine, not sourced): at these phone CPU rates, 2,400 frames take about 55 s with Lite, about 133 s with Full and about 600 s with Heavy. A laptop x86 CPU is likely faster, but this is unmeasured.
2. The model card defines the outputs. Each of the 33 landmarks is (x, y, z, visibility, presence).
   - Visibility, after a sigmoid, is the probability that the point is in frame and not occluded.
   - Presence is the probability that the point is in frame.
   - z is relative to the hip plane, comes from synthetic GHUM fitting, and is "not metric but up to scale".
   - Source: same model card | Google | 2021-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | capability
3. The model card states these limitations:
   - Out of scope: multiple people, subjects farther than about 4 m (14 ft), head not visible, and uses needing metric depth.
   - "When degrading the environment light, noise, motion ... one can expect degradation of quality and increase of 'jittering'."
   - The model is sensitive to face position, scale and orientation.
   - Training data was mostly fitness poses (85K images) and was captured with smartphone back cameras.
   - Source: same model card | Google | 2021-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | capability
4. Model card accuracy is reported as PDJ (PCK@0.2 of torso diameter, which also requires the visibility prediction to match). Averages are Lite 87.0%, Full 91.8% and Heavy 94.2%. The test set is 1,400 smartphone photos from the same source as the training data. The inter-annotator baseline is 97.5%. No sport-specific or golf evaluation is reported.
   - Source: same model card | Google | 2021-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | validation
5. The official MediaPipe Pose Landmarker docs list Lite, Full and Heavy as float16 `.task` bundles. The detector input is 224x224 and the landmarker input is 256x256. There is a VIDEO running mode. The thresholds `min_pose_detection_confidence`, `min_pose_presence_confidence` and `min_tracking_confidence` all default to 0.5. The page gives no latency table.
   - Source: https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker | Google | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | capability
6. rtmlib is licensed Apache-2.0. Its backends are opencv, onnxruntime, openvino and tensorrt, on cpu, cuda or mps.
   - Listed COCO AP: RTMPose-t 65.9, s 69.7, m 74.9, l 76.7 (256x192); RTMO-s/m/l 68.6/72.6/74.8.
   - The README also lists Body26 (Halpe-style, which includes feet) and WholeBody-133 models.
   - Source: https://github.com/Tau-J/rtmlib | Tau-J (GitHub) | unknown (README) | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | licensing / capability
7. MMPose is licensed Apache License 2.0, "Copyright 2018-2020 Open-MMLab".
   - Source: https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/blob/main/LICENSE | OpenMMLab | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | licensing
8. MediaPipe is licensed Apache License 2.0. One subdirectory (a utf utils folder under the language detector) carries a separate Lucent permissive notice that is not relevant to pose. The BlazePose model card is also "Licensed under Apache License, Version 2.0".
   - Source: https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/LICENSE | Google | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | licensing
9. Ingwersen et al. (DTU) compared monocular 3D pose models on golf swings against marker-based motion capture. They found "significant errors" and concluded the models "cannot be used out of the box for advanced golf analytics". The abstract page does not give the models tested or the error numbers.
   - Source: https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/evaluating-current-state-of-monocular-3d-pose-models-for-golf/ | DTU / NLDL 2023 (Septentrio) | 2023-01 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (abstract only) | validation
10. A comparative study of MediaPipe (lite/full/heavy) against YOLOv8 (n to x) measured FPS, CPU and memory. It reports that MediaPipe Lite gives "high FPS and low latency" and that YOLOv8 does better in crowded or occluded scenes. The abstract page gives no FPS numbers and no hardware. The paper is FICAILY 2025, Springer SCI vol. 1229, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-00232-7_13.
    - Source: https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/comparative-evaluation-of-mediapipe-and-yolov8-for-real-time-pose/ | Istanbul Technical University research portal | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low (no numbers) | performance
11. GolfPose (Jiang, Ji, Menaker, Hwang; 2022-07) is described as a temporal 2D pose method that improves golf-club keypoint accuracy over image-based methods. No arXiv ID or dataset release was confirmed.
    - Source: search snippet of a SciSpace listing (https://scispace.com/authors/haorui-ji-3bcurj99e8) | SciSpace (aggregator) | 2022-07 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | validation
12. A US patent on monocular golf pose estimation (12711647) states that the fast club movement causes motion blur, and that the hosel leaves the frame, which "greatly hinder[s]" keypoint prediction. It proposes club bounding-box plus line-segment refinement.
    - Source: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12711647 | USPTO | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | low (search snippet only) | capability
13. In GitHub issue #4895 (google/mediapipe), a commenter states that the newer Task APIs do not support separating left and right landmark groups. This is about API grouping, not about swap errors. No maintainer-confirmed issue on side-view left/right swaps was found.
    - Source: https://github.com/google/mediapipe/issues/4895 | GitHub | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | low (search snippet only) | capability
14. Sportsbox AI: LITSLINK, its development vendor, publishes a case study. It says off-the-shelf pose models were unreliable on outdoor golf footage and describes an on-device model with "17+" landmarks. It gives no error metrics, so this is marketing / vendor register.
    - Source: https://litslink.com/case-studies/ai-golf-swing-analyzer | LITSLINK | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | low (search snippet only) | competitor
15. A validation-method template from outside golf: in JMIR mHealth 2020, a monocular 2D pose app (Lindera) was compared with a multiview reference using joint-angle MAE and ICC. The largest MAE was the right shoulder at 6.48° (SD 8.43°). This is not transferable to golf.
    - Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7781802/ | JMIR / PMC | 2020-12 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (search snippet only) | validation

## Leads worth chasing

- **Measure locally.** No laptop-CPU number for MediaPipe, RTMPose or YOLO-pose was found that is independent of the vendor. A 30-minute timing script on the target laptop (fixed 2,400-frame clip, warm-up, per-frame mean and p95) would give more evidence than further searching. The PRD should state the speed target as a goal to be measured, not a promise.
- **Frame subsampling.** Not sourced; my hypothesis. Analysing 240 fps footage at, say, 60 fps for body pose, and keeping full rate only around impact, may make the 2-minute target achievable even with the Full model.
- **DTU full PDF** (DOI 10.7557/18.6793): it holds the error numbers and the models tested against golf mocap.
- **GolfPose:** find the arXiv or ICPR paper and any dataset release with club keypoints. **CaddieSet** (arXiv 2508.20491): a golf dataset of joint features with ball data and 15 swing metrics.
- **Full text of the FICAILY 2025 MediaPipe-vs-YOLOv8 paper:** it has the FPS and hardware details.
- **Mitigations:** the MMPose benchmark README (read directly) and MediaPipe/RTMPose smoothing options (One Euro filter) still need a primary source.
