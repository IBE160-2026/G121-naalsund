## Claims

1. TPI defines early extension as "any forward movement (thrust) of the lower body towards the golf ball during the downswing." The result is that the arms and club get "stuck" behind the body and the torso rises through the hitting zone. Typical misses are a block right and a hook left. | https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/early-extension | TPI (ACTPI LLC) | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | definition
2. A second TPI article defines early extension as "excessive movement of the pelvis towards the ball (target line) during the swing." It can happen in the backswing or the downswing. TPI calls it a swing *characteristic* rather than a fault, because some elite players (e.g. Jimmy Walker) use it. | https://www.mytpi.com/articles/swing/why-early-extension-causes-a-reduction-of-power-in-the-golf-swing | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | definition
3. TPI's DTL check for early extension draws a vertical reference line behind the tailbone at address (the "tush line" idea, though the article does not use that term). Hips moving off this line toward the ball means early extension. The examples are Walker (moves off the line), Dustin Johnson (stays on it) and Justin James (moves away from the ball). The article is qualitative and gives no cm or degree values. For a 2D rule, this gives a direct proxy: the horizontal (toward-ball) displacement of the hip/pelvis keypoint relative to its address x-position, measured between address and impact. | same URL as #2 | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high (definition) / n/a (numeric) | definition

Another Golf.com piece says "some 70 percent of amateurs … less than 1 percent of touring pros". A Cooper Fitness Center page gives "64.3 percent of golfers". TPI's own pages say only "one of the most common swing characteristics among amateur golfers". 5. TPI defines loss of posture as "any significant alteration from your body's original set up angles during your golf swing." Typical misses are a block right and a hook left, the same as early extension. TPI gives no numeric degree threshold for "significant". | https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/loss-of-posture | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high (definition) | definition
6. TPI defines over the top as overuse of the upper body in the downswing that throws the club outside the intended plane. The club then approaches out-to-in, giving a pull (square face) or a slice (open face). TPI calls it "perhaps the most common swing fault among high handicap golfers" and "one of the leading causes of slicing". No prevalence figure is given. | https://www.mytpi.com/improve-my-game/swing-characteristics/over-the-top | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | definition
7. TPI's DTL ("target line view") test for over the top has four steps. (a) At address, draw a line up the shaft plane, extended past the shoulder. (b) At the point where the lead forearm is just past parallel to the ground in the backswing, draw a second line along the club (grip end to clubhead). (c) The area between the two lines is the "slot". (d) If the club moves above or outside the slot in the early downswing, the player is over the top. This translates directly into a 2D rule. Without club detection, a pose-only proxy is the lead wrist/hands in early downswing sitting above the line from ball/clubhead to the hands at address (hands-only, lower fidelity). | same URL as #6 | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high (method); threshold = geometric (line crossing, 0 px margin) | threshold

Further contributors are restricted lead-hip rotation, limited trunk–pelvis separation (spine/rib-cage mobility, lat length) and weak glutes/core (pelvic control). The linked drills target ankle dorsiflexion (calf) and separation. | URL #1 | TPI | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | fault-chain

### Implications for PRD starting thresholds (synthesis, not sourced numbers)
- **Early extension:** flag when the hip keypoint's toward-ball displacement from address to impact exceeds a starting value. A candidate is ~15 cm (claim 13, single low-confidence source). In 2D, normalise by a body length such as shank or femur length, or the hip–shoulder distance at address, since cm needs calibration. A pixel-free option: express it as a % of the address hip-to-ankle horizontal distance. This must be tuned on labelled clips.
- **Loss of posture:** TPI gives no degree value. A candidate rule is the change in the 2D forward-bend angle (hip→shoulder line vs vertical) from address to impact, and/or head-keypoint rise, flagged above a tuned delta. **No sourced starting number was found.**
- **Over the top:** use TPI's geometric slot rule (claim 7). The threshold is a line crossing, so it needs a club/shaft detector or a hands-only proxy.
