# D1 digest, round 1, researcher 1

**Dimension D1: 2D pose models for down-the-line (DTL) golf video**

Questions owned:
1. Tracking quality of MediaPipe Pose, RTMPose/RTMW and YOLO-pose on wrists, hands and hips during fast motion: motion blur, self-occlusion, side view.
2. Runtime on a laptop CPU, and whether a 10-second clip (300 frames, or 1200 to 2400 at high fps) fits in under 2 minutes.
3. The confidence scores each model outputs, whether they are calibrated, and whether practitioners use them to gate measurements.
4. Licensing basics.
5. How top golf apps do pose estimation (added by the user): 2D vs 3D, on-device vs cloud, camera requirements, published validation.

Note: about 9 sources were read. Some fetches went through a summarising fetch tool, so table figures should be re-checked against the primary page before a PRD quotes them.

## Claims

1. RTMPose CPU latency on an Intel i7-11700 (ONNXRuntime, 1 thread, batch 1, 256x192 input). Pose model alone: t 3.2 ms, s 4.5 ms, m 11.1 ms, l 18.8 ms. Full pipeline with the RTMDet-nano detector: t 12.4 ms, s 16.7 ms, m 26.6 ms, l 36.3 ms. Derived figure: RTMPose-m with the detector runs at about 37 fps on one thread, so 2400 frames take about 65 s and 300 frames about 8 s. This assumes the detector runs on every frame, which is a worst case. | https://arxiv.org/html/2303.07399v2 | arXiv (Jiang et al., RTMPose) | 2023-03 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (one source, a desktop CPU rather than a laptop; needs a second source or a local benchmark) | performance
2. The RTMPose abstract claims RTMPose-m reaches 75.8% AP on COCO at 90+ FPS on an Intel i7-11700 CPU, and RTMPose-s reaches 70+ FPS on a Snapdragon 865. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07399 | arXiv | 2023-03 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high (that the claim is made); medium (that it transfers to laptops) | performance
3. RTMPose uses SimCC: each axis is split into bins and treated as a classification problem with Gaussian soft labels, with a softmax temperature (tau = 0.1). The paper makes no statement that its keypoint scores are calibrated. | https://arxiv.org/html/2303.07399v2 | arXiv | 2023-03 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | capability
4. Ultralytics YOLO26-pose CPU ONNX latency at 640 px input, on a CPU the docs do not name: n 40.3 ms, s 85.3 ms, m 218 ms, l 275 ms, x 565 ms. COCO pose mAP50-95: 57.2, 63.0, 68.8, 70.4, 71.6. Derived figure: YOLO26n handles about 25 fps, so 2400 frames take about 97 s and 300 frames about 12 s. The m size and larger would exceed 2 minutes on 1200+ frames. The current docs page covers only YOLO26, not YOLO11. | https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/pose/ | Ultralytics docs | unknown (current as of 2026) | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (CPU not named) | performance, version
5. YOLO pose returns `keypoints.data` with shape (N, K, 3): x, y and an optional visibility/confidence value. The docs do not discuss calibration. | https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/pose/ | Ultralytics docs | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | capability
6. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker comes in lite, full and heavy variants, all float16. Inputs are 224x224 for the detector and 256x256 for the landmarker. It outputs 33 landmarks as both normalized image coordinates and 3D world coordinates. Configuration includes `min_pose_presence_confidence`. The current docs page gives no CPU latency figures. | https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker | Google AI Edge docs | unknown | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | capability, version
7. BlazePose fps (lite / full / heavy). On a 2019 MacBook Pro (i9 with Radeon Vega 20) in MediaPipe WASM with GPU: 92 / 81 / 38. On an i9-10900K desktop with a GTX 1070: 160 / 140 / 98. On a Pixel 5: 32 / 22. Each keypoint carries a confidence score. For video, the detector runs only on the first frame and tracking handles the rest. These are 2021 browser numbers with GPU help, not pure CPU. | https://blog.tensorflow.org/2021/05/high-fidelity-pose-tracking-with-mediapipe-blazepose-and-tfjs.html | TensorFlow Blog (Google) | 2021-05 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (old, GPU-assisted) | performance
8. Heatmap and peak-based keypoint confidences in standard 2D pose frameworks are poorly calibrated against actual pose accuracy. Closed-form fixes that use instance size and visibility help but do not fully close the gap. A learned add-on, CCNet, improves calibration and raises AP by up to 1.4%. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17105 (ICML 2024, PMLR v235) | arXiv / ICML | 2023-11 (ICML 2024) | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (from abstract and snippets only) | validation
9. Swing Catalyst, a commercial golf and baseball analysis product, says its body-tracking model is "general-purpose ... not specifically optimized for golf". It says the system "has undergone minimal quantitative testing", that "rapid movements and complex poses common in golf swings may ... lead to errors", and that "occluded body points are detected with less precision". It is optimized for face-on and down-the-line views; top-down and back views are not recommended because of occlusion. "Low-resolution, shaky, or blurry videos can significantly reduce accuracy." Inference is 2D only. | https://support.swingcatalyst.com/hc/en-us/articles/18829366835100 | Swing Catalyst | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | competitor, validation
10. Swing Catalyst's markerless capture article is titled as using RTMPose with the RTMDet detector in a top-down setup. A search snippet names RTMPose-X with RTMDet-M via MMPose and 26 keypoints, but the fetched page body did not confirm the model sizes. It tracks the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, head and foot points, and requires a single player who stays in frame. | https://support.swingcatalyst.com/hc/en-us/articles/18829350333468 | Swing Catalyst | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | competitor
11. Sportsbox AI publishes its own validation: single-iPhone 3D compared against AMM3D, a gold-standard motion-analysis system. The test used 30 golfers with one swing each, mixed skill levels, indoor and outdoor, 7-iron and driver. Mean absolute difference was about 2.2° at address, 2.5° at the top and 2.0° at impact for angular metrics (chest and pelvis turn, side bend, forward bend). Linear metrics (sway, lift) differed by 0.6 in at the top and 0.2 in at impact. The cameras were perpendicular to the target line (face-on), with a high-speed shutter to cut motion blur. The page says accuracy depends on camera placement, exposure and frame rate. The page is undated (images dated 2025-01) and not peer-reviewed. | https://help.sportsbox.ai/sportsbox-ai-accuracy | Sportsbox AI | ~2025-01 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (vendor self-validation) | competitor, validation

Monocular 3D pose models evaluated against marker-based mocap of golf swings were "too erroneous to be used for any kinematic analysis" and "cannot be used out of the box for advanced golf analytics". The reprojections looked convincing despite large 3D errors. | https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/evaluating-current-state-of-monocular-3d-pose-models-for-golf/ | DTU / NLDL 2023 | 2023-01 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (abstract only) | validation

## Leads worth chasing
- A second CPU benchmark on a laptop-class CPU for RTMPose through rtmlib/ONNX and for MediaPipe Pose Landmarker in Python (Tasks API, VIDEO mode). The best route is likely the rtmlib README or MMPose deploy benchmarks.
- The MediaPipe BlazePose GHUM model card (goo.gle/3t20GYH), for official per-device latency and per-landmark `visibility`/`presence` definitions.
- GolfPose (Jiang, Ji, Menaker, Hwang, 2022): golf-specific temporal 2D pose that includes the club. It may hold the only golf keypoint accuracy numbers for the wrists and hands.
- CaddieSet (arXiv 2508.20491, CVPRW 2025): golf swing joint features. Check which pose model it used and any error analysis.
- The MediaPipe GitHub issue tracker, to search for "side view" and "occluded arm swap" (left/right arm flipping in profile), a likely DTL failure mode.
- Sportsbox's per-metric Table 2 (an image), whether it validates DTL as well as face-on, and its on-device vs cloud architecture. A LITSLINK case study mentions an on-device model with 17+ landmarks, possibly Sportsbox (unconfirmed).
- RTMW (whole-body, hand keypoints), for whether its hand points stay usable at impact.

## Looked for but not found
- No head-to-head benchmark of MediaPipe vs RTMPose vs YOLO-pose on golf or fast sports footage.
- No pure-CPU fps for the current MediaPipe Pose Landmarker on the official docs page.
- Nothing stating that MediaPipe visibility, RTMPose SimCC scores or YOLO keypoint confidence are calibrated. The ICML 2024 paper argues that such scores generally are not.
- No independent, peer-reviewed validation of Sportsbox AI or any consumer golf app. Not researched in this round: Performance Golf SwingScan, Mustard, SwingCoach AI, Golfboost, V1, OnForm, Hudl Technique.
- No disclosed fps or camera-angle requirements from DeepSwing.
