## Verification

- **V3 (SwingNet 76.1% PCE on GolfDB): VERIFIED for the headline number; per-event figures (impact ~98%, address ~32%) UNVERIFIED by an independent source.** CaddieSet (Kim et al.?, arXiv 2508.20491, 2025-08) reports that fine-tuned SwingNet reaches 78.0% overall, "approximately 2%" above the baseline, and that without Address and Finish the six-event accuracy is 94.1% vs 91.8% for vanilla SwingNet. That is consistent with the 76.1% / 91.8% figures in McNally et al. CaddieSet does not give per-event PCE. The GolfDB repo notes (per search snippet) that the PyTorch release reaches 71.5% PCE without augmentation, so 76.1% depends on augmentation. CaddieSet independently states that Address and Finish are hard to label (subjective, temporally ambiguous), which agrees qualitatively with a low address PCE. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20491
- **V4a (downswing ~0.23-0.30 s): VERIFIED (range ~0.21-0.26 s), from a search snippet of a secondary source.** López de Subijana et al. cite downswings of ~0.23 s (Milburn 1982), 0.21 s (Neal & Wilson 1985) and 0.26 s (Burden et al. 1998), and report 0.23 s downswing / 0.82 s backswing for professionals. The 0.30 s upper bound is not supported by what I retrieved. Source: https://oa.upm.es/2746/ (Spanish; snippet level, not opened)
- **V4b (TPI: >=120 fps, 240 preferred, shutter >=1/1000 s): PARTIALLY VERIFIED / weak.** No independent biomechanics or standards source was found. Independent but low-quality support: a forum report that V1 Sports software recommends a 1/2000 s shutter to prevent club and hand blur, and a Kinovea forum calculation that at 240 fps a 100 mph clubhead moves about 19 cm between frames. The research study Yamamoto et al. 2023 recorded at 240 Hz, and the Acushnet patent mentions 60/120/240 fps. Sources: https://www.kinovea.org/en/forum/viewtopic.php?id=435 ; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1272038/full
- **V5 (64-70% of amateurs early-extend vs <1% of tour players): UNVERIFIED. Origin not found; the figure circulates unattributed.** GOLF.com (James Colgan, 2020-03-02): "It's been estimated that some 70 percent of amateur golfers suffer from some form of early extension ... while less than 1 percent of touring pros suffer from the same affliction." No source is named, and TPI is not mentioned. I found no TPI page or dataset. Treat it as folklore unless TPI primary data turns up. Source: https://www.golf.com/instruction/2020/03/02/chair-fix-problem-plaguing-70-percent-amateur-golfers/

## Claims

1. US12608853B1, "Systems and methods for generating golf screen characteristics". Assignee Acushnet Co (Titleist's parent). Filed 2025-06-24, granted 2026-04-21. Images "may include two-dimensional images or three-dimensional images". 2. Early extension threshold in US12608853: "a distance in front of the player of more than about 6 inches may indicate an early extension swing characteristic"; "between about 3 inches and about 6 inches may indicate a potential early extension". The reference is a vertical line through the rearward-most hip swing point (from the DTL view), assessed during the backswing, transition and/or downswing. Inch-based values "may vary up to about 3 inches". The text found does not say how pixels convert to inches. | same | Google Patents | 2026-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high (text), medium (interpretation of reference) | threshold
3. Loss of posture threshold in US12608853: "over a 10 degree change in one or more of the angles" means loss of posture; 5-10 degrees means potential. The angles come from the head-hip-knee-ankle chain and the ground line, set "at least at address from a down the line perspective". Angle values "may vary up to about 30 degrees". | same | Google Patents | 2026-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | threshold
4. Over the top threshold in US12608853, DTL view: club position at least ~6 inches in front of and above the player, or more than ~3 inches in front of and above the impact plane line, during the downswing near impact, indicates over the top. A shaft-angle criterion of about -10 degrees is also given (the sign is garbled in the extracted text). Qualitative definition: club path starting outside the intended plane. | same | Google Patents | 2026-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium (sign and reference ambiguity) | threshold
5. Other thresholds in US12608853. Sway (DTL): trailing ankle-to-trailing hip line angle vs address below about -3 degrees, with -3 to 0 as potential. Slide (face-on): lead ankle-to-hip line, below about -3 degrees. Hiking: trailing hip point more than ~3 inches above its reference, or an angle below about -2 degrees. | same | Google Patents | 2026-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | threshold
6. US12608853 defines events as P1-P10. P2 is shaft parallel to the ground, P3 is lead upper arm parallel, P4 (top) is detected by the transition into the downswing, P5 is shaft parallel again, P7 is impact (detected by returning to a P1-like pose), and P9-P10 are the finish. | same | Google Patents | 2026-04 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | event-detection
7. Heuristic event detection from the 2D right-wrist trajectory in DTL (sagittal-plane) video. Address is the frame where wrist velocity first exceeds a threshold, minus 30 frames (0.125 s at 240 Hz). Top is the wrist's maximum height. Impact is the wrist's minimum after the top. Finish is the wrist's maximum after impact. A 6 Hz 2nd-order Butterworth low-pass filter was applied. No accuracy is reported for the event detection. | https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1272038/full | Frontiers in Sports and Active Living (Yamamoto et al.) | 2023-11 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | event-detection
8. Yamamoto et al. 2023 used a single DTL camera 3.5 m behind the ball at 240 Hz, 2D HRNet-w48 (17 COCO joints) and DeepLabCut for the club head, with 27 golfers x 20 7-iron trials. "Forward tilt" is defined as the angle of the right ear-to-right hip vector relative to horizontal, from address to impact, roughly 45-60 degrees across participants. The mean tilt did not correlate with score (r = -0.18, n.s.). Within-trial SD of tilt correlated with score (r = 0.595), and so did across-trial variability (r = 0.80). There was no mocap validation. The confidence intervals in the paper appear internally inconsistent. | same | Frontiers | 2023-11 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | definition / validity
9. CaddieSet DTL features, from Faster R-CNN plus HRNet 17 keypoints: SPINE-ANGLE (degrees vs horizontal), HIP-LINE (ratio relative to Address), HIP-ANGLE, RIGHT-DISTANCE (elbow-torso gap ratio), LOWER-ANGLE, arm angles, LEFT-LEG-ANGLE. Features are normalized as ratios relative to address, which avoids pixel-to-inch calibration. SPINE-ANGLE had the strongest DTL correlation with ball speed (r = 0.53). There is no early-extension feature. | https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20491 | arXiv (CaddieSet) | 2025-08 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | definition
10. CaddieSet: SwingNet fine-tuned on GolfDB plus CaddieSet reaches 78.0% PCE on 8 events and 94.1% on 6 events excluding Address and Finish (vanilla: 91.8%). | same | arXiv | 2025-08 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | event-detection
