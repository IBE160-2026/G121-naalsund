## Verdict on differentiators (a)-(d)

- **(a) Cause→effect chains: NOT open as a positioning claim.** SwingCoach AI (#3), PG SwingScan (#15: "chain reaction of symptoms", with early extension named as a symptom) and the PG listing (#13) all market root-cause analysis. Mustard (#21) and AI Golf School (#22) do too. All of these use a *single root fault → symptoms* frame. I found no app that shows an explicit, multi-link chain citing evidence for each step, for example "restricted hip turn (measured X) → early extension (Y) → push/slice". The open space is narrower than the plan assumes: showing the chain transparently and with evidence, not merely naming a root cause.
- **(b) Per-metric confidence or accuracy: largely OPEN.** No app checked shows confidence per metric. The closest is SwingCoach AI's abstention, "says so instead of inventing a number" (#4), which is a binary call/no-call, not a graded confidence level. Swing IQ only gives a generic disclaimer (#10). PG shows precise numbers with no uncertainty (#15). Treat this as an absence finding: I did not check in-app UI, Sportsbox, or Mustard's full listing.
- **(c) Free-form conversational coach grounded in the user's own swing: NOT open.** SwingCoach AI's Coach tab answers "from your own analyses, with dates" (#5). Caddie AI has free-form chat plus Swing History and Swing Memory (#20). AIGolfChat's status is uncertain (#11). All are tiny on traction (single-digit to 18 ratings), so being first at scale or on quality is still possible. Being first with the feature is not.
- **(d) Drill plus mobility/fitness exercise tied to the detected fault: drills are table stakes (every app has them); fitness/mobility tied to the fault is OPEN.** None of the 10 apps checked mentions mobility or fitness exercises (#6, #9, #10, #14, #17, #19, #20). This is the strongest gap found. It also pairs naturally with (a), since body-limitation causes such as hip turn (#21) lead directly to a mobility prescription.
